# Prior-Art Literature Sweep for FRIEDMAN: A Synthetic "Meaning-Shaped Noise" Generator + Designedness-Detector Battery

## TL;DR
- **The individual ingredients of FRIEDMAN are all well-established, but the specific combined system it proposes — a continuously *dialable* synthetic "meaning-shaped noise" generator with known designed-vs-random ground truth, coupled to a *calibrated multi-detector battery scored as ROC/AUC curves at small corpus sizes* — has not been built.** The closest single work is Ashish Nair's April 2026 Indus "synthetic-baseline scorecard," which has the tunable generators and the exact small-corpus domain but uses a percentile/scorecard framework rather than ROC curves, and its generators are anchored to reproducing real corpora rather than exposed as a free "designedness dial."
- **What is heavily trodden:** individual statistical detectors (character/conditional entropy, Zipf/Heaps conformity, long-range mutual-information decay), the Voynich hoax-vs-language debate, the Indus linguistic-vs-nonlinguistic debate, mechanical gibberish generators (Cardan-grille, self-citation), and the well-documented finding that Zipf's law is a weak discriminator.
- **What is genuinely novel (virgin ground):** the *unified* generator-plus-detector-battery as a single calibrated instrument; ROC/AUC calibration of designedness detectors specifically as a function of small corpus size; and a cross-domain reusable benchmark abstraction that would unify Voynich/Indus/SETI/DNA/gibberish detection under one "designedness ROC" testbed.

## Key Findings

1. **Voynich statistics are the most mature battleground and already contain, in scattered form, most of FRIEDMAN's detector primitives and several of its generators** — but always applied to one real artifact, never as a calibrated instrument with ground-truth-labeled synthetic corpora and ROC curves.
2. **The Zipf-law-as-weak-discriminator literature (Miller, Li, Ferrer-i-Cancho) is essentially a proof-of-concept for FRIEDMAN's motivating premise:** simple mechanical processes reproduce headline statistics, so single statistics fail. This is the strongest scientific justification for building FRIEDMAN, and also a caution about which detectors are worth including.
3. **The Indus-script debate independently converged on FRIEDMAN's exact method** — building synthetic non-linguistic baselines and testing whether real corpora can be distinguished from them — most explicitly in Sproat (2014) and Nair (2026). Nair (2026) is the closest prior art overall.
4. **Decipherment researchers (Knight and collaborators) routinely build synthetic ground-truth corpora with tunable length/noise/language, but score decipherment accuracy, never a language-vs-noise detector via ROC.** The generator half of FRIEDMAN has strong precedent; the calibrated-detector-battery-via-ROC half does not, in this domain.
5. **The ROC-based "designedness/artificiality detection" pattern exists — but only in other domains** (bioinformatics coding-vs-noncoding, AI-generated-text detection), never coupled to a statistically dialable meaning-noise generator at inscription scale.
6. **Design-inference theory (Dembski) and its statistical demolition (Elsberry & Shallit; Olofsson) are the essential cautionary literature:** they show precisely how a "design detector" fails (false positives, moving specifications, background-knowledge dependence), which FRIEDMAN must confront directly by measuring false-positive rates via ROC.

## Details

### Area 1 — Voynich manuscript statistical literature (both sides)

**The "genuine language / structure" side.**
- **Montemurro & Zanette (2013), PLOS ONE 8(6):e66344**, "Keywords and Co-Occurrence Patterns in the Voynich Manuscript: An Information-Theoretic Analysis." Used an information-theoretic measure of how much a word's distribution localizes it to text sections; found long-range word-clustering and semantic networks "compatible with those found in real language sequences," and a characteristic informative-segment scale of a few thousand words. Concluded this supports "a genuine message." *Weakness (raised by Rugg and by Timm/Schinner):* non-randomness is not evidence against a hoax, because the proposed hoaxing mechanisms explicitly produce non-random text; the argument attacks a strawman ("hoax = random").
- **Bowern & Lindemann (2021), Annual Review of Linguistics 7:285–308**, "The Linguistics of the Voynich Manuscript." The Yale survey; argues for treating the text as natural language, making statistical arguments about "phonology," morphology, and structure. Explicitly frames the debate as gibberish vs natural language, and encoded-known-language vs unattested-language.
- **Lindemann & Bowern (2020), arXiv:2010.14697**, "Character Entropy in Modern and Historical Texts." Built three comparison corpora (Voynich partitioned by Currier language/scribal hand/transcription; 294 Wikipedia language samples; 18 transcribed historical texts). This corpus-comparison infrastructure is the closest thing in the Voynich world to FRIEDMAN's calibration battery — but comparison corpora are real, not tunable synthetic, and evaluation is descriptive, not ROC. Found H1 (unigram entropy) of Voynichese is unremarkable (3.88–3.94 bits, within corpus range 3.72–4.82), but conditional character entropy (h2) is anomalously low — corroborating Bennett's 1970s finding. (The Reddy & Knight 2011 catalogue of the manuscript records 37,919 word tokens and 8,114 word types.)
- **Reddy & Knight (2011), ACL workshop**, "What We Know About the Voynich Manuscript." Found word-length distributions and conditional letter entropy unlike any natural language but also unlike known ciphers; a two-state HMM behaves as if vowels sit at word-ends. Noted resemblance to an abjad and to Pinyin-transliterated Chinese in predictability.
- **Currier A/B distinction:** the manuscript splits into two statistically distinct "languages"/hands (Currier 1976), reconfirmed with modern methods (e.g., a 2026 arXiv preprint on quantitative confirmation of the Currier distinction).

**The "sophisticated gibberish / hoax" side.**
- **Rugg (2004), Cryptologia 28(1):31–46**, "An Elegant Hoax? A Possible Solution to the Voynich Manuscript," and **Rugg & Taylor (2016), Cryptologia 41(3)**, "Hoaxing statistical features of the Voynich Manuscript." Demonstrated a Cardan-grille + table-of-word-fragments method generating meaningless text that replicates Zipfian word-frequency distribution, symmetric word-length distribution, and non-homogeneous word/syllable distribution. Rugg's key point: "there are ways of producing gibberish which are not random in a statistical sense." *Weaknesses:* chronological anachronism (Cardano's grille postdates the vellum's carbon date by ~a century); Rugg's simpler version fails to reproduce some finer statistics (per critics and the Rugg/Zandbergen follow-ups).
- **Timm & Schinner (2020), Cryptologia 44(1):1–19**, "A possible generating algorithm of the Voynich manuscript" (building on Timm 2014, arXiv:1407.6639). The **"self-citation"/autocopying model**: new words are generated by copying and slightly mutating nearby existing words. A concrete, medievally-executable generator that fulfills both of Zipf's laws and reproduces the co-occurrence/similar-word network. Code released (Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.2531632; GitHub TorstenTimm/SelfCitationTextgenerator). This is the strongest existing single "meaning-shaped noise" generator and the most direct precedent for one FRIEDMAN generator module. **Timm & Schinner (2021), Cryptologia 45(5):434–438** rebut Bowern & Lindemann. **Schinner (2007), Cryptologia** earlier argued from random-walk/repetition statistics that the text is stochastically generated.
- **Zandbergen & Rugg (2021), "The Cardan grille approach ... taken to the next level" (arXiv:2104.12548)** shows the near-binomial word-length distribution could follow from a generic grille-like method.
- **Gaskell & Bowern (2022), Voynich Conference (CEUR Vol-3313)**, "Gibberish after all? Voynichese is statistically similar to human-produced samples of meaningless text" — notably, a member of the Yale (pro-language) group finding Voynichese resembles deliberately-produced human gibberish, illustrating how unsettled the discrimination is.
- **Lisa Fagin Davis** (paleographer, Medieval Academy of America) has emphasized multiple scribal hands and cautioned against premature "solutions"; the commentary hub at ciphermysteries.com (Nick Pelling) documents the field's debate.
- **Recent computational work (2020–2026):** topic modeling (Sterneck, Polish & Bowern), symbol-role/positional analyses (e.g., PMC8794160), and 2026 arXiv preprints on layered positional/directional constraints (Parisel) and the Currier distinction. Machine-learning language-ID attempts (Hauer & Kondrak 2017, "Decoding anagrammed texts") proposed Hebrew as a candidate — widely regarded as inconclusive.

### Area 2 — Statistical tests distinguishing real language from gibberish/glossolalia

- **Zipf's law is a weak discriminator — the central cautionary result.** **Miller (1957)** showed random "monkey typing" (letters + space at random) yields a Zipfian rank-frequency distribution. **Wentian Li (1992), IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 38:1842**, "Random texts exhibit Zipf's-law-like word frequency distribution," proved this analytically (the length→rank transformation stretches an exponential into an approximate power law). **Conrad & Mitzenmacher (2004)** extended it to unequal letter probabilities; **Perline (2015)** to the −1 exponent limit.
- **The counter-argument (Zipf is not vacuous):** **Ferrer-i-Cancho & Solé (2002)**, "Zipf's Law and Random Texts," and **Ferrer-i-Cancho & Elvevåg (2010), PLOS ONE 5(4):e9411**, "Random Texts Do Not Exhibit the Real Zipf's Law-Like Rank Distribution," used three statistical tests to show random-text ranks are statistically inconsistent with real-text ranks. **Howes (1968)** anticipated the critique (random typing's assumptions do not hold for human whole-word production). **Piantadosi (2014), Psychonomic Bulletin & Review**, is the definitive critical review: humans know whole words and produce them intentionally, so subword null models cannot explain the cognitive origin, and random typing is "not even a good statistical model" (poor quantitative fit vs Yule-Simon). **Ferrer-i-Cancho & Gavaldà (2009)** on the intermittent-silence process. **Takeaway for FRIEDMAN:** Zipf-slope alone is a near-useless detector; the discriminating power lives in higher-order structure and in *joint* statistics — exactly what a detector battery must exploit.
- **Long-range correlations / criticality — the higher-order detectors.** **Ebeling & Pöschel (1994), Europhysics Letters 26:241**, and **Montemurro & Pury (2002)** established power-law decay of long-range correlations/mutual information in literary text. **Lin & Tegmark (2017), Entropy 19(7):299** ("Critical Behavior in Physics and Probabilistic Formal Languages"; arXiv:1606.06737) proved Markov/hidden-Markov processes give *exponential* mutual-information decay, whereas natural language and context-free grammars can give *power-law* decay — and proposed "a simple quantitative test of any generative model": hallucinate text and measure mutual information vs separation. This is arguably FRIEDMAN's single most important detector, and its theory directly predicts which generator classes (regular vs context-free vs recursive/deep) can fool it. Caveats: **Takahashi & Tanaka-Ishii (2017/2019)** argue two-point mutual information imperfectly captures long-range dependence; the 2025 "L2M" work (arXiv:2503.04725) argues two-point MI is inadequate vs bipartite MI.
- **Glossolalia — a real-world "meaning-shaped noise" corpus.** **Samarin (1972), *Tongues of Men and Angels*** and **Goodman (1972), *Speaking in Tongues*** established glossolalia as phonologically structured but semantically empty ("neither internally organized nor systematically related to the world"), typically echoing the speaker's native phonology, with reduced phoneme inventory and open-syllable preference. **Link & Tomaschek (2023), *Language and Speech* (doi:10.1177/00238309231163170)** found glossolalic syllable sequences show a Zipfian pattern like natural language, with shorter syllables at higher probabilities. **Motley (1982)** psycholinguistic analysis. This is a natural validation corpus for FRIEDMAN: human-produced meaningful-sounding gibberish with known "no meaning" ground truth.
- **Gibberish / AI-text detection (NLP).** Practical detectors: Markov-chain character-bigram scorers (rrenaud), DistilBERT 4-class gibberish detectors (madhurjindal, Macro-F1 ~0.97). LLM-text detection supplies the generator+detector+AUC template: **GLTR (Gehrmann et al. 2019)**, **DetectGPT (Mitchell et al. 2023)** (probability-curvature), **Binoculars (Hans et al. 2024)** (perplexity/cross-perplexity ratio), evaluated by AUROC. These are structurally the FRIEDMAN pattern but the "generator" is a fluent LLM and the task is human-vs-machine, not language-vs-designed-noise at inscription scale.

### Area 3 — Synthetic languages / ciphered corpora as decipherment benchmarks

- **Kevin Knight's decipherment program:** Knight & Yamada (1999) "A computational approach to deciphering unknown scripts"; **Knight, Nair, Rathod & Yamada (2006), COLING/ACL**, "Unsupervised Analysis for Decipherment Problems" (EM-based); Ravi & Knight (2008, 2009, 2011) Bayesian/n-gram decipherment; **Knight, Megyesi & Schaefer (2011), ACL BUCC**, "The Copiale Cipher" (a 105-page, ~75,000-character book using 90 distinct cipher symbols, deciphered as an 18th-century German "Oculist" secret-society ritual manual via frequency/HMM clustering). These use synthetic ciphers with known plaintext for evaluation, and **Nuhn & Knight (2014), EMNLP**, "Cipher Type Detection," is the closest Knight-camp analog to a *detector* — but it classifies cipher type, not language-vs-designed-noise via ROC.
- **Barzilay/Luo (MIT) decipherment and evaluation methodology:** **Luo, Cao & Barzilay (2019), ACL P19-1303**, "Neural Decipherment via Minimum-Cost Flow: From Ugaritic to Linear B" — evaluates on cognate-translation accuracy against known ground truth (67.3% of Linear B cognates; +5.5% on Ugaritic in the noisy setting). **Luo, Hartmann, Santus, Barzilay & Cao (2021), TACL 9:69–81**, "Deciphering Undersegmented Ancient Scripts Using Phonetic Prior" — evaluates on Gothic/Ugaritic (deciphered) and Iberian (undeciphered). **Snyder, Barzilay & Knight (2010), ACL**, "A Statistical Model for Lost Language Decipherment."
- **Tunable synthetic ground-truth corpora in decipherment:** Aldarrab & May (2022, arXiv:2205.12527) built 100 synthetic English ciphers of fixed length 2,048 mimicking real DECRYPT ciphers; Aldarrab's USC thesis reports language-ID on 100K synthetic ciphers; a 2026 attention-LSTM homophonic-decipherment paper (arXiv:2606.05078) sweeps synthetic ciphertexts across languages, centuries, text lengths, and noise. **Verdict:** the *generator* half of FRIEDMAN (synthetic corpora, known ground truth, tunable length/noise) is well-precedented in decipherment; the evaluation target is always decipherment accuracy, never a calibrated detector ROC.
- **Statistical-law model evaluation:** **Takahashi & Tanaka-Ishii (2019), Computational Linguistics (J19-3003)**, "Evaluating Computational Language Models with Scaling Properties of Natural Language," evaluates n-gram/PCFG/Simon–Pitman-Yor/RNN/LSTM/GAN generators against Zipf, Heaps, Ebeling, Taylor, and long-range-correlation laws. This is the richest existing toolkit for the statistical-property axes FRIEDMAN wants to dial (and documents that most generators fail long-range correlation) — but it judges model realism, not designedness, and is not small-corpus or ROC-based.

### Area 4 — Undeciphered scripts beyond Voynich

- **Indus script — the debate that independently reinvented FRIEDMAN's method.**
  - **Rao, Yadav, Vahia, Joglekar, Adhikari & Mahadevan (2009), Science 324(5931):1165 (DOI 10.1126/science.1170391)**, "Entropic Evidence for Linguistic Structure in the Indus Script": "the script's conditional entropy is closer to those of natural languages than various types of nonlinguistic systems" (comparisons: English, Sumerian, Old Tamil, Sanskrit vs DNA, protein sequences, and the programming language Fortran); plus **Yadav et al. (2010), PLOS ONE 5(3):e9506** (n-grams) and a **PNAS (2009) Markov model**. **Rao (2010), IEEE Computer** generalized to block entropy.
  - **Farmer, Sproat & Witzel (2004), Electronic Journal of Vedic Studies 11(2):19–57**, "The Collapse of the Indus-Script Thesis": the nonlinguistic-symbol-system thesis, resting on four pillars — inscriptions too short, no long repeated formulae, high singleton-sign rate, heraldic/emblematic distribution. **Farmer, Sproat & Witzel (2009)**, "A Refutation of the Claimed Refutation..." (safarmer.com/Refutation3.pdf), attacked Rao's artificial control datasets as "invented."
  - **The Computational Linguistics exchange (2010, vol. 36(4)):** **Sproat (2010), "Ancient Symbols, Computational Linguistics, and the Reviewing Practices of the General Science Journals"** — showed conditional entropy alone fails to discriminate (constructed Zipfian counterexamples with conditional independence match on that one statistic); **Rao et al. (2010), "Entropy, the Indus Script, and Language: A Reply to R. Sproat"** (with NSB Bayesian block-entropy estimation for small samples) and a parallel reply by Lee et al. **Sproat (2014), *Language* 90(2):457–481**, "A statistical comparison of written language and nonlinguistic symbol systems," is the mature statement: single statistics do not discriminate when proper *attested* controls (heraldry, kudurrus, etc.) are used.
  - **Nair (2026), arXiv:2604.17828**, "How Non-Linguistic Is the Indus Sign System? A Synthetic-Baseline Scorecard" — **the closest prior art to FRIEDMAN.** Builds two *parameterized* non-linguistic generators (a heraldic-emblem generator with tunable Zipf exponent, opener/closer positional classes, bigram preferred-successor rate; and a template-based administrative generator), sweeps parameters across empirical ranges calibrated to six attested non-linguistic corpora, and tests whether the Indus corpus can be distinguished on four FSW-derived metrics simultaneously. The corpus is "1,916 deduplicated inscriptions comprising 11,110 sign tokens and 584 unique sign types ... range from 2 to 17 signs in length (mean 4.4, median 4.0, σ = 2.0)," drawn from the ICIT database (52 sites; raw corpus 2,511 inscriptions, with 595 exact duplicates (24%) removed). It finds the Indus corpus occupies an intermediate position matching neither baseline cleanly, and replicates "a Zipf slope of −1.49 and a conditional entropy of 3.23 bits" (Sec. 5.2: 3.232 bits vs a shuffle-null mean of 4.613 ± 0.015 bits, percentile 0.000 across 1,000 permutation trials). **Crucially, it uses a percentile/scorecard framework, NOT ROC/AUC, and its generators are anchored to reproducing real corpora rather than exposed as a continuous designedness dial.** A follow-on, arXiv:2608.02999 (2026), argues statistical measures are non-specific for decipherment.
- **Linear A / Bologna INSCRIBE (Silvia Ferrara's ERC group).** **Corazza, Ferrara, Montecchi, Tamburini & Valério (2021), Journal of Archaeological Science 125:105214**, "The mathematical values of fraction signs in the Linear A script: A computational, statistical and typological approach" — used constraint programming/optimization to assign values (binary fractions 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 plus sexagesimal-compatible 1/10…1/60) to undeciphered fraction signs. INSCRIBE also produced work on Cretan Hieroglyphic sign-list rationalization, numeracy origins (Valério & Ferrara 2022, Historia Mathematica), and rongorongo radiocarbon dating (Ferrara et al. 2024, Scientific Reports; Berlin/Rapa Nui tablets suggesting possible independent invention). This is computational epigraphy on structure with partial ground truth, not a synthetic-generator + detector benchmark.
- **Rongorongo.** Statistical/structural analyses (Pozdniakov & Pozdniakov 2007 on glyph-distribution matching Rapanui syllables; Horley; **Melka (2009), Cryptologia 33:24–73** on the Santiago Staff; Melka (2008) on Keiti) find non-random, language-like distribution but no decipherment. The corpus is tiny — roughly 15,000 glyphs across only about two dozen surviving objects (the INSCRIBE/Pozdniakov figure; some 2026 counts give 27 tablets and over 400 glyphs) — the extreme small-N regime FRIEDMAN targets.
- **Phaistos Disc.** Timm (2005) and others have produced statistical arguments; the disc's 241 sign-tokens (122 on Side A, 119 on Side B, comprising 45 distinct signs in 61 sequences) make it the canonical too-small-to-decide case — Elizabeth Barber (*Archaeological Decipherment*, Princeton UP, 1974) held that "there is not enough statistical information" to decipher a script with only 241 nonalphabetic signs.
- **Meroitic.** Smith (2008, arXiv:0808.2904) investigated the Zipf plot of extinct Meroitic — an example of applying the weak Zipf discriminator to a real undeciphered corpus.

### Area 5 — Design / intentionality detection more broadly

- **SETI / animal communication.** **McCowan, Hanser & Doyle (1999), Animal Behaviour 57:409–419** applied Zipf/Shannon-entropy tools to bottlenose-dolphin whistles; **Doyle, McCowan, Johnston & Hanser (2011), Acta Astronautica 68:406–417** ("Information theory, animal communication, and the search for extraterrestrial intelligence") proposed higher-order entropy and Zipf slope as "detectors and estimators for possible extraterrestrial intelligent signals," explicitly treating Zipf conformity as a necessary-not-sufficient "language fingerprint." **Ferrer-i-Cancho & McCowan (2009), Entropy 11:688** ("A Law of Word Meaning in Dolphin Whistle Types"). **Elliott (2000), COLING C00-2150**, "Language Identification in Unknown Signals," proposes detecting language-like structure in unknown signals via Zipfian type-token and segmentation — the FRIEDMAN detection goal, with no synthetic benchmark or ROC. A 2025 exploratory paper (arXiv:2506.02730) proposes LLM "generative reactivity" as a SETI structure detector (explicitly speculative). These are conceptual cousins: designedness via Zipf/entropy, but no tunable generator and no ROC calibration.
- **Archaeology — artifacts vs geofacts.** The lithic-analysis literature (criteria for distinguishing deliberately knapped stone tools from naturally fractured "geofacts": bulbs of percussion, platform regularity, flake-scar patterning) is the archaeological analog of design detection. It is largely qualitative/checklist-based rather than information-theoretic, but conceptually parallels FRIEDMAN's "designedness" target and offers a domain where false positives (seeing tools in nature) are a known hazard.
- **Dembski's design inference — essential cautionary/contrast case.** **Dembski (1998), *The Design Inference* (Cambridge UP)** and **(2002), *No Free Lunch*** proposed "specified complexity"/CSI and a "Law of Conservation of Information" as a design detector. Statistical demolitions: **Elsberry & Shallit (2011), Synthese 178(2):237–270**, "Information theory, evolutionary computation, and Dembski's 'complex specified information'" (CSI is ill-defined, the filter yields false positives, background-knowledge dependence, no rigorous LCI proof); **Fitelson, Stephens & Sober (1999)**, "How not to detect design"; **Olofsson (2008), Biology & Philosophy 23:545**, "Intelligent design and mathematical statistics: a troubled alliance"; **Häggström (2007)** on the NFL theorems; Perakh (2004). **Lesson for FRIEDMAN:** a design detector is only meaningful if its false-positive rate is measured against realistic chance/mechanical alternatives — which is exactly what an ROC framework over calibrated synthetic generators provides, and exactly what Dembski's filter lacked.
- **Algorithmic information theory / randomness deficiency.** Kolmogorov complexity and **Martin-Löf (1966) randomness** (a sequence is random iff it passes all effective statistical tests, equivalently is incompressible: K(x↾n) ≥ n − c) formalize "structure = compressibility = randomness deficiency." Compression ratio is thus a principled (though uncomputable-in-the-limit) designedness detector, and practical compressors (LZMA, as in the ZipPy AI-text detector) approximate it. **Devine (2014), Zygon 49(1):42**, and an arXiv AIT critique (0901.0399) apply AIT directly to Dembski's arguments. This grounds a compression-based detector module for FRIEDMAN.

### Area 6 — Generative models with dialable structure

- **PCFG / hierarchical generation:** Lin & Tegmark (2017) show context-free grammars reproduce power-law mutual-information decay that Markov/n-gram models cannot — so a PCFG module with a tunable branching/recursion parameter is the natural "high-realism" knob for FRIEDMAN, and its output is theoretically predicted to fool the mutual-information detector that flat Markov generators cannot.
- **Markov / n-gram generation:** the Lin & Tegmark diagnostic (hallucinate + measure MI) and Takahashi & Tanaka-Ishii's scaling-law battery precisely document what n-gram/Markov generators do (Zipf, Heaps) and do not (long-range correlation, criticality) reproduce.
- **Self-citation / autocopying:** Timm & Schinner's released generator is a ready-made module producing text that satisfies both Zipf laws and Voynich-like similar-word networks.
- **Cardan-grille / table-and-grille:** Rugg & Taylor's method is a second ready-made mechanical generator with documented reproduction of word-length and frequency statistics.
- **Existing open-source tunable synthetic-corpus tooling:** none found that is purpose-built as a tunable-meaning-noise generator paired with an ROC-scored detector battery. The nearest reusable pieces are Timm's self-citation code, Nair's (2026) heraldic/administrative generators (publicly released), and Takahashi & Tanaka-Ishii's scaling-property evaluation suite.

### Gap analysis: which components of FRIEDMAN are well-trodden, partial, or novel

**Well-trodden (do not reinvent; integrate):**
- Individual detectors: unigram/conditional character entropy (Bennett; Lindemann & Bowern), Zipf/Heaps conformity, long-range mutual-information decay (Ebeling & Pöschel; Lin & Tegmark), block entropy (Rao), Yule's K/Rényi entropy (Tanaka-Ishii).
- The finding that Zipf's law is a weak discriminator (Miller, Li, Ferrer-i-Cancho).
- Individual mechanical generators: Cardan-grille (Rugg), self-citation (Timm & Schinner), Markov/n-gram, PCFG.
- The demonstration that non-randomness ≠ meaning (the entire Voynich hoax literature).

**Partially explored:**
- Synthetic non-linguistic baselines with *tunable* parameters tested against a real corpus: Nair (2026) for Indus; Sproat (2014) with attested (non-synthetic) controls; Rao et al.'s (2009) artificial controls.
- Synthetic ground-truth corpora with tunable length/noise: the Knight/Aldarrab/Barzilay decipherment benchmarks (but scored on decipherment accuracy).
- A statistical-property "battery" applied to generators: Takahashi & Tanaka-Ishii (2019) (but for realism, not designedness, and not small-N or ROC).
- ROC/AUC evaluation of a designedness/artificiality detector: bioinformatics coding-vs-noncoding (Liu, Gough & Rost 2006, PLOS Genetics, entropy-feature SVM with ROC) and AI-text detection (AUROC) — but never with a tunable meaning-noise generator.

**Genuinely novel (virgin ground):**
1. **The unified instrument:** a single system that *jointly* exposes a continuously dialable meaning-noise generator (entropy rate, Zipf slope, morphology, positional/line regularities, long-range correlation as independent knobs) with known designed-vs-random ground truth spanning a continuum, and a calibrated detector battery. Every existing instance has one leg or the other, never both as one calibrated tool.
2. **ROC/AUC of designedness detectors as an explicit function of small corpus size.** The small-N degradation of statistics is *noted* (Rao's NSB estimator; bioinformatics short-sequence work; Nair) but never systematically mapped as detector ROC-vs-N. This is FRIEDMAN's sharpest novel contribution and directly serviceable to real inscription corpora (rongorongo ~15,000 glyphs; the Phaistos disc's 241 signs; Indus mean length 4.4 signs).
3. **Charting the "fooling frontier":** systematically locating, per detector and per corpus size, the generator parameter settings at which mechanical processes begin to fool each detector — a phase-diagram of detectability that no existing work produces.
4. **A cross-domain reusable "designedness ROC" benchmark** unifying Voynich/Indus/SETI/DNA/gibberish. Every current instance is siloed to one domain.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Establish the gap formally and cheaply (0–3 months).** Reproduce three published results as unit tests before building anything new: (a) Lin & Tegmark's MI-decay diagnostic on Markov vs PCFG output; (b) the Zipf-slope collapse (Miller/Li random typing vs real text) to confirm your battery correctly rates Zipf as weak; (c) Nair's (2026) Indus scorecard, then re-express it as ROC. If you cannot re-derive Nair's discrimination as an AUC, that itself is the paper: "designedness detection needs ROC, not scorecards." *Benchmark to proceed:* your battery must rank detectors by AUC consistently with the literature's qualitative claims (entropy-h2 and MI-decay strong; Zipf-slope weak).

**Stage 2 — Build the generator with independently dialable strata (3–9 months).** Implement, at minimum, four generator modules with a *shared* statistical-target interface: (i) Markov/n-gram, (ii) PCFG with tunable recursion (the long-range-correlation knob), (iii) Timm–Schinner self-citation, (iv) Cardan-grille. Expose entropy rate, Zipf slope, morphology (affix inventory), positional/line constraints, and MI-decay exponent as target parameters, and *verify* each generated corpus hits its targets (Nair's own generators overshoot/undershoot targets — treat target-fidelity as a first-class metric). *Benchmark to proceed:* generators reproduce their commanded statistics within a stated tolerance across the parameter sweep.

**Stage 3 — Calibrate the detector battery as ROC vs corpus size (9–15 months).** For each detector and each corpus size N (spanning Phaistos-scale ~200 tokens to Voynich-scale ~38,000 tokens), compute ROC/AUC for the designed-vs-random discrimination, and locate the "fooling frontier." Include a compression-ratio (LZMA) detector as the AIT-grounded module and the Lin–Tegmark MI-decay detector as the theoretically-favored one. *Benchmark:* a publishable phase-diagram of AUC(detector, generator-parameters, N).

**Stage 4 — Validate against real held-out corpora with known status (15–18 months).** Test on (a) known-meaningful text (natural languages, deciphered Linear B/Ugaritic), (b) known-meaningless human gibberish (glossolalia transcripts; Rugg/Timm generated text), and (c) the contested cases (Voynich, Indus, rongorongo). Report where each real corpus falls on the calibrated ROC surface rather than issuing a binary verdict.

**What would change these recommendations:** If a systematic search turns up an existing tool that already couples a tunable generator to ROC-scored detectors (the targeted search found none in any domain), pivot from "build" to "extend/benchmark against it." If the fooling frontier turns out to be trivially low (even simple generators fool everything at inscription scale), the contribution shifts from "detector battery" to a negative result — "designedness is undecidable below N tokens" — which is itself high-value for the Voynich/Indus/rongorongo debates.

## Caveats
- **Source-quality flags:** much of the Voynich debate lives in a specialist journal (Cryptologia), conference proceedings (CEUR Vol-3313), preprints (arXiv), and well-regarded technical blogs (Nick Pelling's ciphermysteries.com; Rugg's hydeandrugg; René Zandbergen's voynich.nu); these are where the field's argument actually happened, but they are not all peer-reviewed to the same standard. I have prioritized the peer-reviewed core (PLOS ONE, Science, Annual Review of Linguistics, Cryptologia, Computational Linguistics, Language) and flagged blogs as such.
- **The closest prior art (Nair 2026, arXiv:2604.17828) is a recent unrefereed preprint by an independent researcher;** its details (ROC-absent scorecard, tunable heraldic/administrative generators, Indus statistics) should be independently verified against the published version if one appears. Its own generators imperfectly reproduce target statistics — a limitation, not a fatal flaw, and one FRIEDMAN should avoid.
- **Recency/verification:** several 2026 arXiv items (Currier-distinction confirmation, Parisel positional-constraint paper, arXiv:2608.02999, homophonic-decipherment LSTM) are very new and may change on revision.
- **Speculative sources explicitly marked:** the SETI "generative reactivity" paper (arXiv:2506.02730) is exploratory/forward-looking, and popular-science coverage of Doyle's Whale-SETI work (Nautilus, Big Think, Space.com) overstates settled conclusions; the peer-reviewed McCowan/Doyle papers are the reliable anchor.
- **The negative finding** ("no tool literally combining a tunable meaning-noise generator with an ROC-scored detector battery in any domain") is a bounded literature search, not a proof of non-existence; FRIEDMAN's novelty claim should be stated as "no such combined system was found in a thorough sweep" rather than as certainty.
- **Dembski is included only as a methodological cautionary/contrast case**, not as endorsed science; the intelligent-design program is peer-reviewed-rejected pseudoscience, and its relevance to FRIEDMAN is strictly the demonstration of how a design detector fails without false-positive calibration.
